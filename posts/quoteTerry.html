The EE vs. Computer Science Debate

A Humorous & Technical Investigation into Engineering's Eternal Rivalry

"Everybody knows electrical is higher in the engineering pecking order than CS because it requires real math ;-)"
——Terry A. Davis (2011)

1. The Famous Claim & The Rivalry

The debate between Electrical Engineering (EE) and Computer Science (CS) represents one of academia and industry's most enduring cultural divides. On one side, electrical engineers view themselves as guardians of physical reality, wrestling with Maxwell's equations and semiconductor quantum mechanics. On the other side, computer scientists command virtual realms of pure abstraction, orchestrating algorithms that power modern civilization.

When TempleOS creator Terry A. Davis famously quipped that EE sits higher in the "pecking order" due to "real math," he was tapping into a long-standing banter. But is EE math genuinely more "real," or do the two disciplines simply explore fundamental reality through fundamentally different mathematical lenses?

2. Why Electrical Engineering Feels "More Real"

Electrical engineering is directly bound to physical laws. Electrons, electromagnetic fields, thermal noise, and signal degradation exist in the physical universe and obey dynamic, continuous rules.

The Continuous Mathematical Toolkit of EE

  • Differential Equations & Vector Calculus: Used to model field interactions, wave propagation, and transient circuit states.
  • Fourier & Laplace Transforms: Converts complex continuous time-domain signals into the frequency domain for filter design and communications.
  • Maxwell's Equations: The foundation of classical electromagnetism, describing how electric and magnetic fields interact.
  • Semiconductor Physics: Applies quantum mechanics to govern PN junctions, MOSFET gates, and electron mobility.

In EE, mathematical errors carry instant physical consequences:

V(t) = L (di / dt) + R i(t) + (1 / C) ∫ i(t) dt

If an engineer miscalculates feedback stability or power dissipation, the circuit doesn't display a stack trace or throw a runtime exception—it fails physically, overheats, or explodes.

3. The Myth of CS "Fake Math": Discrete & Algorithmic Rigor

Dismissing Computer Science math as "fake" fundamentally misunderstands the field. Computer science does not model physical matter; it models computation, logic, and information structure.

The Theoretical Foundations of CS

  • Discrete Mathematics & Graph Theory: Used to model network routing, compilers, databases, and structural dependencies.
  • Automata & Computability Theory: Explores what problems can actually be solved by a machine (Turing Completeness, Halting Problem).
  • Complexity Theory (P vs. NP): Quantifies the fundamental time and spatial bounds required to compute solutions.
  • Information Theory & Cryptography: Uses number theory, abstract algebra, and probability to secure digital communication (e.g., RSA, Elliptic Curves, Shannon Entropy).

Computer science math operates in the realm of provable absolute logic:

O(N \log N) \quad \text{vs.} \quad O(N^2) \quad \implies \quad \text{Fundamental Limits of Algorithmic Efficiency}

A algorithmically flawed software design won't burn a circuit board, but it can crash global banking infrastructure, stall spacecraft, or take billions of years to terminate execution.

4. The Convergence: Computer Engineering & Embedded Systems

The divide between EE and CS is bridged by Computer Engineering (CpE). This field lives precisely where physical electronics transform into logical execution.

Software Applications & Algorithmic Design Pure CS

Instruction Set Architecture (ISA) & x86/ARM Assembly CpE Bridge
Digital Logic Gates, Registers & VLSI Design CpE / EE
Transistors, MOSFETs & Silicon Physics Pure EE

6. The Terry Davis Philosophy

Terry Davis's perspective was rooted in a radical bottom-up understanding of computing. Building TempleOS completely from scratch—writing his own kernel, compiler, graphics library, and 64-bit operating system—required intimate knowledge of how software interacts directly with hardware registers and CPU interrupts.

From this low-level worldview, modern software development often appears buried under endless layers of bloated abstractions, frameworks, and external dependencies. The philosophy posits:

"The closer you are to the hardware and the electrons, the deeper your understanding of computing reality."

7. Final Verdict: Continuous Physics vs. Abstract Logic

Is Electrical Engineering "higher" in the pecking order? Not objectively.

EE holds supremacy in modeling the physical mechanics of universe hardware. CS holds supremacy in modeling the logical limits of information and problem-solving.

The modern technological landscape depends entirely on both disciplines functioning in total synchronization. The ultimate engineer is not the one who claims hierarchy over another, but the one who can navigate seamlessly across the entire spectrum:

Electrons ➔ Semiconductors ➔ Logic Gates ➔ Architecture ➔ Operating Systems ➔ Algorithms ➔ Software
